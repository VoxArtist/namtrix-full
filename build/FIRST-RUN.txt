NAMTRIX Full — read this once
=============================

1. Drag "NAMTRIX Full.app" to your Applications folder.

2. The first time, RIGHT-CLICK it and choose "Open" — do not double-click.
   macOS will ask whether you are sure. Say yes.

   On some versions of macOS this is enough. On others, right-click → Open
   still refuses and just shows the same "cannot be opened" message a plain
   double-click would. If that happens, use the System Settings route below —
   it always works.

   IF RIGHT-CLICK → OPEN DOESN'T WORK:

     a. Try to open the app once (double-click, or right-click → Open).
        It will refuse. That refusal is expected — it's what unlocks the
        next step.
     b. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
     c. Scroll down to the Security section near the bottom of the page.
        You'll see a line like:
            "NAMTRIX Full" was blocked to protect your Mac.
     d. Click "Open Anyway" next to it.
     e. You may be asked for your Mac password or Touch ID. Confirm.
     f. Open the app again (double-click is fine now). One more dialog
        asks you to confirm — click "Open".

   None of this means the app is unsafe. It means the app isn't notarized by
   Apple, which requires a paid Apple Developer account ($99/year). Every
   unsigned or ad-hoc-signed app hits this same wall — it's not specific to
   NAMTRIX. You only do this once per Mac; macOS remembers after that.

3. macOS will ask for microphone permission. Say yes.

   It counts any audio input as a microphone, including your interface. If you
   say no, recordings come back silent instead of giving you an error, which is
   a confusing way to lose an hour. You can change it later in
   System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone.

That is the whole installation. Nothing else to install — the app carries its
own Python and audio libraries.

Double-click it and your browser opens on the tool. Quit from the Dock when you
are done.

If you extract the zip from a terminal, use "ditto -x -k" rather than "unzip":
unzip does not preserve everything the app's signature covers, and macOS will
then refuse to run it.
